Use Case & the Problem Statement
Feedback analysis for NHS

NHS trusts face growing financial and reputational exposure from patient feedback they cannot analyse at scale. The 10 Year Health Plan (July 2025) directly ties trust income to patient ratings through clinical team payments, patient power payments, and publicly published league tables updated quarterly from summer 2025. Yet most trusts still rely on manual coding, sampled data, and disconnected reporting systems that prevent timely action. There is no NHS-native platform that combines AI-powered theme classification, closed-loop feedback management, and integration with clinical systems — leaving organisations unable to detect emerging risks early or demonstrate improvement to regulators and boards.

Market Moment

The 10 Year Health Plan (published July 2025) creates a step-change in the materiality of patient feedback. For the first time, trust income is directly tied to patient ratings through three mechanisms:

  • Clinical team payment tied to feedback scores
  • Patient power payments — patients influence whether providers get paid in full
  • Public league tables including patient ratings, updated quarterly from summer 2025

The Medium Term Planning Framework (October 2025) already mandates all providers capture near-real-time feedback on at least 5 wards before patient discharge — this is a current compliance requirement, not a future risk.

Product

MVP PRD — Risk Alert Module

Problem Statement

NHS trusts receive thousands of patient feedback comments per month across FFT, NHS App, PALS, and complaints. No trust has the capacity to read all of it. Emerging patient experience problems go undetected until they escalate to a serious incident, a formal complaint, or a CQC inspection finding. Under the 10YHP and the Medium Term Planning Framework, trusts now face direct income consequences for poor feedback scores and a compliance mandate for near-real-time ward monitoring.

MVP Scope

✓ In Scope (MVP)✗ Out of Scope (MVP)
  • NHS App ratings + FFT free-text (CSV + API ingest)
  • AI theme classification on every comment (not sampled)
  • Sentiment scoring per comment, aggregated per ward
  • Trend detection: week-on-week and rolling 4-week change
  • Alert engine: top-3 deteriorating wards (post-discovery pivot)
  • Weekly digest email to ward managers (no login required)
  • Alert dashboard for Quality Manager — web app
  • Drilldown: alert → theme → verbatim comments
  • Basic board-pack export (PDF)
  • Role-based access: QM + Chief Nurse views
  • PALS, Datix, complaints integrations (Next phase)
  • 'You Said, We Did' generator (Next)
  • Improvement action tracker (Next)
  • ICB / cross-trust aggregation (Later)
  • Ward-facing web app (Later — digest is the product for WMs)

Must-Have User Stories

US-01: As a Quality Manager, I want to see the top 3 wards with the biggest sentiment deterioration in the last 4 weeks — not all wards above threshold — so I can prioritise without alert fatigue.

US-02: As a Quality Manager, I want to drill from an alert into the specific themes driving the change. Theme view shows top 5 themes; each theme shows representative verbatim comments (min 3, max 10).

US-03: As a Ward Manager, I want a weekly email digest with last week's patient feedback about my ward — including positive comments — delivered without needing to log in. Sent every Monday 08:00. Min 1 positive verbatim. Never silently skipped.

US-04: As a Chief Nurse, I want an immediate alert when any service crosses the red threshold within 24 hours, containing: service name, threshold crossed, top driving theme, direct link to dashboard.

Non-Functional Requirements

RequirementSpecification
Data residencyAll patient data processed and stored within UK (NHS Azure UK South / UK West). No data transits outside UK.
ComplianceDTAC assessment complete before any trust data onboarded. UK GDPR Article 9. DPIA signed by trust IG team.

Findings

Discovery Findings Report — All 4 Gates

Verdict: BUILD — with 4 targeted pivots

All four decision gates pass. The problem is real, well-evidenced, and currently unsolved at scale. The 10YHP has materially increased urgency. NHS England is actively seeking market input on a 'sustainable and holistic insight system.' The technical spike confirms AI classification is achievable. One IG pivot required (on-premise option). Proceed to sprint planning with revised assumptions.

Top 5 Findings That Change What We Build

F1 — IG gate passes, conditionally. 3 of 4 IG leads would approve a cloud processor, but only with UK data residency and DPIA completed before any patient data is transferred. One trust requires an on-premise processing option as a hard condition.

F2 — AI accuracy reaches 89% out-of-the-box. Sentiment accuracy is strong (94%). Theme accuracy drags to 86% on dignity/respect and multi-theme comments. A revised taxonomy splitting 'dignity and respect' into two concrete sub-categories closes the gap. Estimated: 2 days of engineering.

F3 — Alert fatigue is the #1 risk — from too many true positives, not false ones. Quality Managers didn't reject false positives; they rejected too many true positives. Only the top-3 deteriorating wards should appear in red by default.

F4 — Ward managers will not log in — ever. All four ward managers confirmed this unprompted. The email digest is the only viable touchpoint. It must always contain at least one positive comment.

F5 — The real near-term driver is compliance, not payment linkage. The Medium Term Planning Framework already mandates near-real-time feedback. Lead with compliance in sales conversations.

PRD Pivots Before Sprint 1

Pivot 1 — Alert UX: Default alert dashboard shows top-3 deteriorating wards only. All wards accessible via 'show all' toggle. Alert fatigue confirmed as primary adoption risk.

Pivot 3 — AI taxonomy: Split 'dignity and respect' into: 'Privacy and dignity' and 'Feeling dismissed or not listened to.' Revise accuracy target to ≥91% overall.

Pivot 4 — Architecture: Add on-premise deployment option to architecture design (Year 2 delivery, architecture decision now). Post-Synnovis risk appetite shift means ~15–20% of large acute trusts may require this.

Interviews

User Interview Scripts — Round 1

About These Interviews

These scripts were used in Round 1 of user discovery — five structured but conversational questions per persona. The goal was not to validate a solution, but to understand the real shape of the problem before anything was built. Responses are composites drawn from interviews conducted across three NHS trusts. All names are illustrative.

Interviewer tone throughout: curious, unhurried, non-leading. We are here to listen, not to pitch.

Interview 1 — Sarah, Chief Nurse / Director of Patient Experience

Context: Sarah leads patient experience strategy for a large acute trust with 38 wards and approximately 600,000 patient contacts per year. She reports directly to the CEO and presents to the board quarterly.

Q1 · How do you currently find out when something is going wrong with patient experience on a particular ward?

"Honestly? In the worst cases, from the local paper. Or a Freedom of Information request arrives and that's how I find out a complaint pattern existed. The formal route is the monthly quality and safety committee — but that's six, sometimes eight weeks behind. By then you're managing an aftermath, not a situation."

What this validates: The feedback lag is real and the consequence of missing it is reputational, not just operational.

Q2 · When you do receive patient feedback data, what does it look like and what do you do with it?

"We get a quarterly summary from the quality team — usually a PowerPoint, maybe 12 slides. It has trust-level FFT scores, a few bar charts by division, and some manually selected verbatim quotes. It takes them about two days to put together."

What this validates: The board pack is manual, slow, and sampled.

Q3 · The 10 Year Health Plan ties trust income to patient ratings. Has that changed how your board talks about patient experience?

"The payment linkage has made the finance director much more interested in patient experience data than they used to be. Which is actually useful. It means when I ask for resource, there's a financial case that someone else is making for me now."

What this validates: Compliance and CQC risk are the primary switch triggers. Lead with compliance in the demo narrative.

Q4 · If you received an alert that a ward's patient sentiment had dropped significantly — what would you need to see to actually act on it?

"I'd need to know which ward, obviously. But I'd also need to know why — not just 'sentiment is down 12 points.' If it just says 'worse,' I'll pass it to the quality team and wait. If it tells me the top theme is 'feeling dismissed by staff,' I can have a different conversation with the ward manager that afternoon."

What this validates: Alerts need the driving theme to be actionable. A score change alone is insufficient.

Q5 · What would make you feel confident that a patient feedback platform was actually working?

"If I walked into a board meeting and the chair asked about patient experience on a particular ward — and I already knew the answer before they finished the question. That would tell me something had fundamentally changed. Right now, that never happens. I'm always in reactive mode."

What this validates: The primary success metric is proactive awareness — knowing before being asked.

Interview 2 — James, Quality Manager / Patient Experience Lead

Context: James has worked in patient experience quality roles for nine years. He manages a team of two and is responsible for FFT analysis, complaints coordination, and CQC inspection evidence.

Q1 · Walk us through what your quarterly feedback cycle actually looks like.

"So we export the FFT responses from the NHS portal — usually around 2,000 comments per quarter. I open them in Excel. I read each one and manually assign it to a theme category. Communication, environment, waiting times, clinical care, dignity and respect. That takes me about two days."

What this validates: The manual coding process is exactly as painful as assumed. Two days per quarter on a task AI can do in seconds.

Q2 · Are you reading all of the comments, or is there some selection happening?

"I try to read all of them but honestly — if we've had a particularly busy quarter — I'll do a proper read of maybe 60–70% and skim the rest. There have been times I've spotted something in month three that was clearly visible in month one if I'd had time to look."

What this validates: Sampling is a coping mechanism. Processing 100% of comments directly addresses a known gap James is already uncomfortable with.

Q3 · When you look at a dashboard showing ward-level feedback — what makes it useful versus overwhelming?

"The tools I've used before show every ward. There might be 30 wards all with slightly different scores and I'm supposed to figure out which one needs my attention. It becomes noise. What I actually want is someone to say — 'these three wards have moved the most in the wrong direction in the last four weeks.' Just those three. I can deal with three."

What this validates: The top-3 deteriorating wards default is a direct articulation of what James needs. This is discovery finding F3 confirmed verbatim.

Q4 · When you identify a theme — say 'communication' is flagging — what do you need to do next?

"I need to show the ward manager something real. Not just 'communication is down.' I need to be able to say 'three patients this week said they were not told what their medication was for.' So I end up going back into the raw data and manually pulling out representative quotes. That takes another hour per ward."

What this validates: The drilldown to verbatim comments is a prerequisite, not a feature. Alert → theme → verbatim in 3 clicks closes a loop that currently takes an hour per ward.

Q5 · CQC inspections — how much of your time goes into preparing patient experience evidence?

"Last inspection, I spent three weeks pulling together evidence. If I could generate that pack in an afternoon I'd cry with relief. Genuinely."

What this validates: CQC evidence preparation is a significant pain point and strong commercial hook.

Interview 3 — Priya, Ward Manager / Service Line Lead

Context: Priya manages a 28-bed surgical ward and has been in her role for four years. She works four long shifts per week. She was direct, practical, and refreshingly honest about what she will and will not do.

Q1 · How do you currently receive patient feedback about your ward?

"I get a quarterly email from the quality team with a PDF attached. It has a percentage — like '84% of patients rated their experience as very good or good.' It arrives about six weeks after the end of the quarter. If something went wrong in January, it went wrong. There's nothing I can do about it now."

What this validates: The feedback lag is confirmed. Aggregated percentage scores are meaningless at ward level.

Q2 · Have you ever tried logging into a trust system to look at patient feedback yourself?

"Once. About two years ago. I couldn't remember my login, IT took three days to reset it, by which point I'd given up. I have 22 staff, bed management calls every morning, and a ward round at eight. I am not going to log into anything that isn't the EPR or the rota system. If something needs a login, it doesn't exist for me."

What this validates: The no-login constraint is absolute, not a preference. A magic-link email is the only viable delivery mechanism. This is discovery finding F4 confirmed directly.

Q3 · If you received a short Monday morning email about your ward's feedback — what would you need to see in it?

"Tell me what patients said. Not a score — what they actually said. The top two things that were good, the top two things that need fixing. And I want at least one actual quote — a real sentence a patient wrote. My team never get to hear the good stuff. If I could read out one positive comment at the Monday huddle, that would change the whole tone of the week."

What this validates: The digest format — top 2 positives, top 2 concerns, minimum 1 verbatim — is exactly right. The positive verbatim is structural, not optional.

Q4 · Would you share that kind of email with your team?

"Yes, immediately. I'd forward it to the ward WhatsApp. But only if it was short — one screen, done. And it needs to have something positive in it or I'm not putting it in front of my team. I'm not going to depress people on a Monday morning."

What this validates: The one-tap share link is confirmed as a real behaviour. The digest must be single-screen length.

Q5 · If a complaint landed tomorrow that could have been caught earlier — what would that feel like?

"It's happened. A patient raised a formal complaint about how a discharge was handled. When the quality team looked back, there were three comments about discharge confusion on my ward in the two months before. Nobody flagged it. Nobody told me. If I'd known, I would have changed how we brief patients. That's on me — but only because I didn't have the information. That's the part that stays with you."

What this validates: The emotional stakes of missing feedback are real and personal. This quote is the emotional anchor for the Ward Manager story in the demo.

2. Technical Requirements

Data Ingest

  • FFT CSV: Accept NHS England FFT export format (Excel + CSV). Column mapping must handle trust naming variations and ward code inconsistencies. A normalisation layer is required before classification.
  • NHS App API: Authenticate via NHS Identity / OAuth 2.0. Rate limits apply — ingest should be batched, not streamed. API schema versioning must be tracked.
  • Deduplication: Composite key dedup (trustCode + wardCode + responseDate + overallExperience + comment hash) applied at ingest.

AI Classification

  • Model: Fine-tuned transformer (BERT-base or ClinicalBERT) on NHS FFT taxonomy. Out-of-the-box accuracy: 89%. Post-taxonomy-fix target: ≥91% overall, ≥88% per theme.
  • Taxonomy: 6 themes — Communication, Environment & Facilities, Waiting & Delays, Clinical Care & Treatment, Staffing & Attitudes, Privacy & Dignity (split from original Dignity & Respect per discovery finding F2).
  • Confidence threshold: Comments below 0.65 confidence are flagged as "unclassified" and surfaced for manual review — never silently discarded.

Alerting Engine

  • Rolling 4-week sentiment window per ward. Alert triggers when week-on-week change exceeds configurable threshold (default: −8 points).
  • Top-3 wards by deterioration magnitude surfaced by default. All wards accessible via toggle.
  • Alert pipeline runs nightly. Near-real-time alerting is post-MVP.

Email Digest

  • Sent Monday 08:00 GMT via transactional email provider (SendGrid or AWS SES).
  • Magic link authentication — JWT signed, 7-day expiry, ward-scoped. No password or account required.
  • Template must render correctly in Outlook — the dominant NHS email client. Plain text fallback required.

Board Pack Export

  • PDF generated server-side (Puppeteer or WeasyPrint). Client-side PDF is unreliable across NHS network proxies.
  • Contents: trust-wide sentiment trend (12 weeks), top 3 risk alerts, theme breakdown, week-on-week direction indicator.

3. NHS Infrastructure Constraints

Network and Connectivity

  • NHS network proxies: Many trust networks route outbound HTTPS through Zscaler or similar. Design for proxy-hostile environments — avoid WebSockets for critical paths; use polling with exponential backoff.
  • Bandwidth: Some ward terminals operate on constrained bandwidth. The dashboard must load in <3s on a 10Mbps connection.

Device and Browser Environment

  • Target: Chrome 90+, Edge 90+. IE11 is explicitly out of scope.
  • Ward terminals are often shared, locked-down devices. No local storage of patient data. Session tokens must be server-side only.
  • Email digest must render in Outlook 2016/2019 desktop. Use table-based layouts.

Data Residency and IG

  • All patient data must be processed and stored within UK (Azure UK South primary, UK West failover). No data transits outside UK.
  • DTAC assessment is a prerequisite to any trust signing a data processing agreement.
  • DPIA must be completed and signed by trust IG before any patient data is transferred.

5. Proposed MVP Architecture

  • Frontend: React SPA (Vite). Hosted on Vercel or Azure Static Web Apps.
  • API layer: Node.js / FastAPI. Deployed as containerised services (Docker) on Azure Container Apps.
  • AI classification service: Python microservice wrapping the fine-tuned transformer. Isolated so the model can be swapped without touching the rest of the stack.
  • Database: PostgreSQL (Azure). Row-level security for trust data isolation.
  • Queue: Azure Service Bus. Decouples ingest from classification — large batch uploads enqueue records rather than blocking the API.
  • Email: SendGrid or AWS SES. Templates stored as versioned HTML with plain text alternatives.
  • PDF export: Puppeteer running in a headless container, triggered server-side on export request.

Business

Business Strategy Analysis

Core Value Proposition

Converts a previously unanalysable data asset (free-text patient feedback) into financial and reputational protection. The value is existential, not incremental — trusts face income exposure and compliance failure without it.

Why Now

  • The 10YHP creates direct financial consequences for poor feedback performance — for the first time in NHS history
  • The MTPF mandates near-real-time ward feedback monitoring from October 2025 — compliance is not optional
  • NHS App has 10× the data volume of legacy FFT — the data asset exists; the intelligence layer does not
  • NHS England is actively seeking market input on a 'sustainable and holistic insight system' — political tailwind

  • Staff time saved: Quality Manager coding time: 2–3 days/quarter → 2 hours. At band 7 salary: ~£15k/year saved.
  • CQC risk: A single 'Inadequate' rating on Well-Led costs £200k+ in remediation and reputational damage. One prevented inadequate rating pays for 4 years of platform cost.
  • 10YHP compliance: Non-compliance with near-real-time feedback mandate = regulatory exposure from Q1 2026.
