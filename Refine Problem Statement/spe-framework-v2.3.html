The shift

The traditional lifecycle asks: how quickly can we build?

This framework asks

How can we prove this idea is worth delivering — before we build it?

Success is how early a weak idea dies, not how many ideas reach delivery. The aim is to fail earlier, not more often — when the cost of being wrong is lowest.

What the five dimensions mean

Governance usually asks whether an artefact exists: is the business case written, is the design approved. These are confidence gates instead of document gates — they ask how much we know.

Problem
Is it real, and whose is it?
Desirability
Will the people who have it actually use this?
Value
Is it worth more than it costs?
Feasibility
Can it be built, with the data that legally exists?
Operational
Can it be run, supported and paid for?
Why we keep a learning log
Not a record of failure
An idea that failed on cost or technology this year may be viable next year. The log is how we will know it is worth revisiting.
Organisational memory
Before a new case tests an assumption, the log is searched. Teams stop quietly re-running each other's experiments.
Success is a kill rate
Measured by how quickly a weak idea is eliminated, not by how many ideas reach delivery.

Method

Building organisational confidence and memory

The learning log is a first-class asset, not a by-product. Every gate decision, every discarded option and every failed experiment becomes memory the next decision can draw on.

The overarching proposition

Build confidence before commitment. Learn continuously. Keep humans accountable.

AI accelerates every stage — sensing opportunities, testing assumptions, building, operating and learning. Humans provide the intent, judgement and accountability that turn those capabilities into responsible decisions and measurable value.

The five gates

These are confidence gates, not document gates. Conventional governance checks whether an artefact exists — business case written, requirements signed, design approved. Each gate here asks something harder: is there enough evidence to justify the next level of commitment? Nothing advances on documentation alone, and investment rises only after confidence does.

The principle

We do not eliminate uncertainty. We manage it deliberately, using evidence to increase confidence before increasing commitment — so a weak idea is stopped at the cheapest gate it reaches, not the most expensive one.

The learning log

Every decision creates organisational memory; every experiment makes the next decision better. One log runs the length of the lifecycle and answers five questions, so a failed experiment becomes an asset rather than waste — and so AI has something honest to learn from when the next opportunity arrives.
